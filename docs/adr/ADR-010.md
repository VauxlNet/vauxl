# ADR-010: Compliance Baseline — DSGVO (German) Primary, GDPR Global

**Status:** Accepted
**Date:** 2026-01-01

## Context

Vauxl operates under German jurisdiction. German data protection law (DSGVO /
BDSG) imposes stricter requirements than the GDPR baseline in several areas:
data minimisation, logging retention, right to deletion, and the requirement
for a public Impressum. Enterprises evaluating Vauxl will audit compliance
before deployment.

## Decision

Adopt the German DSGVO (GDPR implementation in Germany) as the primary
compliance baseline. All design decisions default to DSGVO compliance first,
then extend to GDPR compliance for international deployments. Specific
requirements include: data subject access (Art. 15 DSGVO), right to erasure
(Art. 17), data portability (Art. 20), and logging retention limits (no
longer than necessary per purpose).

## Consequences

- Positive: DSGVO compliance implies GDPR compliance — simpler audit story.
- Positive: data minimisation by default reduces attack surface.
- Positive: clear regulatory framework for design decisions (e.g., retention,
  deletion, export).
- Negative: stricter logging rules than some other jurisdictions require.
- Negative: some features (e.g., message retention policies) must be designed
  with deletion guarantees from day one.
- Negative: legal advice is required before public launch — not replaceable by
  technical measures alone.

## Alternatives Considered

- **GDPR baseline only**: risk of non-compliance with German-specific
  requirements (Impressum, BDSG).
- **US-centric (CCPA)**: weaker protections, misaligned with Vauxl's privacy
  mission.
